# OctoAcme — Project Management Overview

OctoAcme runs projects with a clear, staged lifecycle and lightweight artifacts that keep work aligned and discoverable. Initiation starts with a Project One‑pager that captures the problem, SMART objectives, success metrics, stakeholders, and a high‑level timeline; once success metrics and stakeholder agreement are confirmed the project moves into planning. Planning breaks approved initiatives into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, a Definition of Done, and a release/milestone map. Execution follows an iterative cadence — kickoffs, timeboxed sprints, regular demos, and releases — and ends with a close and retrospective to capture learnings and action items.

The team uses pragmatic workflows and tooling to manage day‑to‑day delivery. A project board (Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, Done) is the single source of truth for task state, and Pull Requests follow a disciplined process: smaller PRs where possible, include an issue link and acceptance criteria, run automated tests and linting in CI, and require at least one approval prior to merging. Releases are classified as patch, minor, or major and require pre‑release checks (CI, security scans, release notes, and a rollback plan) plus staged verification (staging smoke tests and post‑deploy checks).

Roles and responsibilities are explicitly defined to ensure clear ownership. Product Managers set outcomes and prioritize the backlog; Project Managers manage schedules, risks, and stakeholder coordination; Developers implement, test, and document changes; and QA validates acceptance criteria through a mix of automated and manual testing. Communication cadence includes daily standups for execution-level coordination, weekly delivery syncs and PM/PdM alignment, monthly stakeholder updates, and clear escalation paths for urgent issues.

Quality assurance and risk practices are embedded across the lifecycle. QA expectations include unit and integration tests, end‑to‑end smoke tests for critical flows, CI security scanning, and manual QA for acceptance where needed. Risks are tracked in a register (ID, impact, likelihood, owner, mitigation) and reviewed in weekly syncs; escalation follows team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor, with separate procedures for security incidents. Retrospectives convert top learnings into prioritized action items that feed back into the backlog to drive continuous improvement.
